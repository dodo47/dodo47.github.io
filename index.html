Hello there!

I am a Principal Investigator at the Faculty of Mathematics, University of Vienna.
In my research, I develop theoretically grounded and resource-efficient machine learning methods, with a particular focus on spiking neural networks, neuromorphic computing, quantum machine learning, and autonomous, self-organising systems for space. I am a passionate mentor and communicator, with a lot of experience in managing research projects.
A highlight of my career is co-founding ESA's new "SPAICE: AI in and for Space" conference, which I still organise as a scientific chair. I also had the honor of writing viewpoint articles both for the American and German Physical Society!

Selected projects

Current Principal Investigator · ESA OSIP · 2026–

xQML for Space-Compatible Quantum Chips

This project develops explainable quantum machine learning algorithms for space applications. A major goal is to ensure that these methods are compatible with emerging photonic quantum chips. A particular focus lies on data reuploading models: a class of expressive models that operates with as little as a single qubit.

Completed Principal Investigator · MSCA · 2024–2026

Biologically-inspired Autonomous Systems for Space Exploration (BASE)

As principal investigator of the MSCA fellowship project BASE, I investigated biologically inspired approaches to autonomous systems from both a theoretical and a physical perspective. One direction focused on developing new theory for spiking neural networks to understand their expressivity. The second explored self-organising and reconfigurable space infrastructure, including modular cube ensembles and Totimorphic lattices.

Completed Research Fellow · ESA ACT · 2021–2024

AI Research at ESA’s Advanced Concepts Team

I conducted independent research in ESA's Think Tank, the Advanced Concepts Team, leading work on spiking neural networks for onboard AI, material inverse design, as well as neuromorphic and memristor-based AI accelerators. In addition, I co-organised scientific competitions for the GECCO conference, and helped establish a new ESA conference on AI and space.
